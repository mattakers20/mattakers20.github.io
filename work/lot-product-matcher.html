Showing builders exactly which homes fit which lots

Two internal tools, a site-plan designer and a product catalogue, combined into a matcher that computes each lot’s buildable area and shows which house plans fit it.

Organization
Sifton Properties, Neighbourhood Developments
Role
Sole designer and builder
Timeline
2026
Stack
HTML, JavaScript, rules engine; no server
Data in
Site-plan lot geometry; product catalogue
Volume
Lot frontage, depth, setbacks, density class; plan footprints

Selling lots to builders starts with one question: what can I build here?

Each new neighbourhood is laid out as a site plan of lots with different frontages, depths, prices, and density classes. Builders choose lots with specific house and building plans in mind, so every conversation depends on matching the two.

Lot inventory lived in static PDFs and spreadsheets, so every fit question meant manual back-and-forth

  • Site plans were shared as PDFs that could not show status, price, or dimensions interactively.
  • A lot’s buildable area depends on zoning setbacks, so fit could not be read from the lot size alone.
  • Matching plans to lots was done by hand, one conversation at a time.

A matcher that nets out setbacks and answers the fit question instantly. Try it.

Pick a house plan. Lots that can take it fill in; lots that cannot say why.

House plan (width × depth)

Site-plan designer. Traces a neighbourhood’s site plan from a PDF or image and lets the team draw lots, roads, and amenities over it, tagging each lot with status, price, dimensions, and density class.

Product catalogue. Sifton’s house and building plans, with the dimensions the matcher needs.

Matcher. Computes each lot’s buildable frontage and depth net of zoning setbacks, then determines which plans fit by density class and dimensions, as a live match view and a builder-facing export.

Zero infrastructure. Exports are self-contained web pages: no server and no developer involvement to publish a new neighbourhood.

Builder conversations start from what is buildable, not from a static PDF

  • Interactive, self-contained pages replace static PDFs and spreadsheets as the way lot inventory is shared.
  • Fit between lots and plans is computed rather than argued, streamlining builder conversations about what goes where.
  • New neighbourhoods publish without a server or a developer.
