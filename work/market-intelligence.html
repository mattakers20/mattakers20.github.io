Leadership needs an early read on every regulatory, industry, and competitor move in southwestern Ontario

Land development decisions turn on news that arrives from many directions at once: municipal planning changes, provincial housing policy, industry reports, and what competing developers are announcing. The team owns competitor tracking for the region and maintains the market-intelligence picture senior leadership works from.

Twenty-one sources, each with its own format, took hours of reading to monitor by hand

  • Sources published on different schedules, in inconsistent formats, often repeating the same story.
  • Most items were irrelevant to the team, but finding the few that mattered meant reading all of them.
  • Past coverage was effectively lost: answering “what have competitors said about this?” meant searching from scratch.

An automated pipeline that collects, cleans, and ranks, with a question-answering layer on top

  1. 1Collect

    21 regulatory, industry, and competitor sources, pulled daily

  2. 2Clean

    Normalization and de-duplication canonicalize entries across sources

  3. 3Filter

    A cheap keyword stage removes clearly irrelevant items

  4. 4Score

    An LLM stage scores what remains for relevance to the team

  5. 5Brief

    A ranked digest goes to senior leadership

  6. 6Ask

    RAG answers ad-hoc questions over the archive, with citations

Why two scoring stages. Running every item through a language model is slow and costly, and keywords alone miss context. The keyword stage clears the obvious noise so the LLM spends its effort only on items that might matter.

Why data quality came first. The same announcement often appears in several sources. Normalization and de-duplication run before scoring, so leadership never sees one story ranked three times, and flagged results are validated before they reach the digest.

Why a question layer. The digest answers “what happened today.” The retrieval-augmented assistant answers “what do we know,” searching months of collected coverage and citing the articles each answer came from.
