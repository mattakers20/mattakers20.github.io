A developer’s lots are only as safe as the builders who put homes on them

Before engaging a builder, the team needs to know whether that builder is licensed, whether it has faced convictions, insolvency filings, or regulatory notices, and how its warranty and claims history compares. Ontario’s home-building regulator publishes much of this through its public builder directory.

The records were scattered, point-in-time, and unfair to large builders when read as raw counts

  • Scattered. Licence status, convictions, insolvency filings, notices of proposal, and claims history each sit in a different part of the record.
  • Point-in-time. The directory shows the current state; it does not say what changed since you last looked.
  • Misleading counts. A builder with hundreds of homes will have more claims than one with a few dozen, even if its homes are more reliable.

One register that ingests the records, scores them as rates, and diffs every run against the last

Ingestion. The tool pulls the regulator’s public licensing data into a local database: licence status, convictions, insolvency filings, notices of proposal, and warranty and claims history.

Weighted scoring. Each factor carries a weight, and claims are normalized as rates rather than raw counts, so larger builders are not penalized for scale.

Change detection. Each crawl is compared with the one before it. New licences, status changes, insolvency flags, and new convictions or notices are surfaced as changes, so reviewers see what moved rather than re-reading the whole register.

Delivery. Results appear in an HTML “Builder Risk Register” dashboard the team can open without installing anything.
